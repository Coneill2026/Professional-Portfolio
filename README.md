# Professional Portfolio Template

A reusable, responsive portfolio template built with HTML, CSS, and TypeScript. The website contains placeholders only. This README holds the assessment guide and AI declaration template so those instructions do not appear on the public-facing site.

## Run locally

1. Install Node.js if it is not already installed.
2. In this folder, run `npm install`.
3. Run `npm run build` to compile `src/main.ts` to `assets/main.js`.
4. Open `index.html` in a browser, or use VS Code's Live Server extension.

Re-run the build after changing the TypeScript. The site is static and needs no server-side code.

## Personalise the website

- Replace all bracketed placeholders with accurate details in your own professional voice.
- Add a professional photograph and useful project images or screenshots. Replace the placeholder LinkedIn, email, CV, and optional GitHub links.
- Keep the required LinkedIn link visible, and add professional contact details and a CV/resume if appropriate.
- Replace, duplicate, or remove project cards to show your own work. Make sure each project explains your contribution and links to relevant evidence.
- Add appropriate image descriptions, check that links work, and review keyboard access, contrast, mobile layout, and print/PDF output.
- Delete the copy of any placeholder or personal files you do not want to include in your final portfolio folder.

## Assessment guide

The brief recommends **1,500–2,000 words total**, excluding evidence, screenshots, images, links, CV, and appendices. It recommends concise, reflective writing supported by relevant evidence rather than long essays.

| Portfolio section | Weight | Recommended content |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| About Me (homepage) | 20% | 150–250 words. Professional biography, key skills, career interests, professional networking details, visible LinkedIn link, professional photograph, and consistent personal presentation. A CV/resume is optional but encouraged. |
| Learn More | 10% | 250–350 words. Professional identity, strengths, aspirations, and three important values, supported by examples from workshops, teamwork, academic projects, or personal experience. Reflect on why the values matter and how they shape teamwork and career choices. |
| What I Learned From Others | 20% | At least four speaker, industry-talk, employer-event, or other professional-engagement reflections. The brief recommends 450–600 words total and also estimates about 150–200 words per reflection; these figures conflict, so confirm with your lecturer. Discuss key messages, what surprised or challenged you, and how the learning may influence career decisions. Evidence can include notes, photos, screenshots, LinkedIn connections, or event materials. |
| Professional & Industry (Career Connect) | 10% | 200–300 words. Reflect on expectations, learning about employers/residency, memorable conversations, and actions you will take. Support it with event photographs, notes, employer discussions, or networking evidence. |
| Beyond the Classroom | 15% | 250–350 words. Show active citizenship and community contribution through relevant activities such as volunteering, peer mentoring, societies, hackathons, leadership, class representation, or outreach. Reflect on your contribution, impact on others, and personal development. Evidence may include photos, certificates, testimonials, participation records, or project outputs. |
| How I Developed This Year | 15% | 250–350 words. Reflect on significant academic and professional development using situation, learning, application, and next step. Include evidence of completing both the Library Skills Programme and Assignment Toolkit; other evidence may include workshops, training, certifications, goals, technical projects, or team learning. |
| Where Am I Going Next? | 10% | 200–300 words. Set three to five realistic, measurable goals for the next 12 months. Explain actions, target dates, and how you will measure success. Reflect on your evolving professional identity, the impression you want to make on employers, and areas of your profile still to develop. |
| Reading and resource list | — | Include five to ten relevant books, podcasts, professional blogs, industry newsletters, technical documentation, websites/courses, career resources, or community events. Add brief annotations; the list is excluded from the word count. |

Projects is an optional showcase between Learn More and What I Learned From Others. The website numbers every section in its display order; the weighted rubric still covers the seven required sections listed above.

The section-level word recommendations do not add up consistently to the total, and the speaker-reflection estimate is also inconsistent. Prioritise thoughtful reflection over padding and check with your lecturer which guidance to follow.

### Top-band qualities

- **Reflection:** demonstrate self-awareness and critical insight; connect experiences, learning, and future development.
- **Evidence:** use relevant artefacts, examples, and experiences to support your reflections.
- **Professional development:** show a mature understanding of professional expectations, career planning, and personal development.
- **Organisation and navigation:** make the portfolio logically organised, intuitive, and easy to navigate.
- **Presentation and branding:** create a polished, consistent, accessible online presence that expresses your professional identity.

### Submission checklist

- Keep the LinkedIn link visible on the portfolio.
- Submit the website URL through the Microsoft Form.
- Upload a PDF archive copy to Brightspace.
- Check the portfolio on desktop and mobile, test all links, and preview the print/PDF version.

## AI declaration and prompt record

The brief requires a statement at the end of the manuscript for any AI or AI-assisted tools used. Keep the declaration here rather than on the website, then include this completed README or copy the final statement into the accompanying submission appendix/PDF so the assessor receives it.

### Declaration of Generative AI and AI-assisted technologies

> I used **[tool name and version]** to **[explain why and how you used it]**. It assisted with **[identify the relevant parts of the portfolio]**. I reviewed and adapted its output by **[explain how you checked and changed it]**. **[Add any required acknowledgement or citations.]**

Include AI assistance used to create or adapt this template. If you did not use AI for any part of your own portfolio, state that accurately instead.

Keep a separate prompt-and-response record for **every** AI interaction: date, tool/version, complete prompt, complete response, and how the output was used. Retain the full record for academic-integrity checks and include it as an appendix if required by the brief.
